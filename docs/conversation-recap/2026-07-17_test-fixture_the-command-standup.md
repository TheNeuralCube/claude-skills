PREVIOUSLY ON: "THE COMMAND"

A comedian, blazer, brick wall.

"You ever have a friend come to you drowning, and instead of throwing him a rope, you give him homework? 'Hey man, I'm struggling at work.' 'Great news. Run this command and get back to me.' He asked for a lifeguard. You sent him a form."

Scene: a phone buzzes, 9:45 AM.

Richard is drowning. Juggling things while juggling other things, can't remember the details of the day. He reaches out. He's vulnerable. He says the words "I'm interested," which for Richard is practically a love letter.

And what does our guy send back? Does he say "that sounds rough, buddy"? No. He sends a text the length of a lease agreement. The man cried for help and received a keynote. There's a pitch in there! "Hoping to scale it out to our users at work." His friend is going under for the third time and this guy sees a customer segment.

Then, the dinner. Richard says: busy tonight, but tomorrow night, and listen to this, I'LL BUY. A free dinner. Offered. Tomorrow. Time-stamped generosity. And our guy replies, "How about sometime next week?"

WHO COUNTERS A FREE DINNER? You don't negotiate a free dinner! A free dinner is a cab with the light on, you get in! Instead the dinner now lives in "sometime." Nothing in "sometime" has ever happened. "Sometime" is where plans go to be remembered fondly at funerals.

Then the confession. "FYI, I actually dont have a laptop." Next sentence: "Well, I actually do." Which is it, Richard?! You can't be laptop-agnostic! Turns out it's an old Apple that he hates, and here's the tragedy, it cannot run his "duel monitors." Duel. D-U-E-L. Two monitors at dawn, ten paces, turn and display.

And our hero's condolence, this is a masterpiece: "That's weird that your Apple can't do that. Mine did but that's OK." MINE DID. That's not tech support, that's a flex wearing a sympathy card. "Sorry for your loss, my grandmother lived to a hundred."

Now the interrogation. "What's your budget?" Richard hesitates. The follow-up comes in, one word: "Range." RANGE. Like a loan officer! This is a friendship being run like a mortgage application. Two decades of brotherhood and he's pulling a credit report.

Richard responds with a dissertation. It's not that he HAS a budget, it's about usage frequency, it's about what he needed to do. He won't be gaming, but he will be doing "AI." In quotes. He put AI in quotes! Like it's alleged. The so-called AI. Allegedly intelligent.

The verdict lands: Apple is best for the AI stuff. But Richard is allergic to Apple. Carries an EpiPen past the Genius Bar. Then the greatest surrender in the history of consumer electronics: "I guess I could suck it up."

The man waves the white flag. And how is he consoled? "Just get it updated to the latest OS. And get me the specs. I'll get you a command to run on it." He surrendered and got assigned chores! You handed a defeated man a mop!

And Richard ratifies this entire treaty how? One thumbs-up tapback. An entire Geneva Convention, signed by emoji.

Final scene. The command goes out into the night. One line, no symbols, lovingly idiot-proofed, with a screenshot fallback, because deep down, our guy knows exactly who he's dealing with.

Back to the comedian.

"And the specs? Never came back. Of course they didn't! You gave a grown man homework at 10:31 on a weekday. And the dinner? Still 'sometime.' Fifty years from now, two old men shuffle past Cafe Brussels. 'We should really do that dinner.' 'Yeah. Sometime next week.'"

WHERE WE LEFT OFF: Specs outstanding. OS update unverified. Dinner unscheduled, still theoretically Richard's treat. Season two hinges on one question: is the hated old Apple secretly good enough, or does this end with Richard in a Best Buy, holding a Windows box, whispering "freedom"?
